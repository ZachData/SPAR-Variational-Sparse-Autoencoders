# Confirmatory battery — results, 2026-09-03

Companion to `FINDINGS_2026-09-02.md` (what was wrong with the pilot) and
`REMEDIATION.md` (what was done about it). This file records **what the corrected
battery measured**.

Design: 6 seeds per arm on gelu-1l, `d=2048`, `k=256`, layer 3 residual stream,
10k steps. Liveness measured over 1,000,192 streamed samples per checkpoint.
Every p-value below is `0.00216`, which is the **exact two-sided minimum for 6v6** —
it is the design floor, not a measure of effect size. Read the `d` column.

---

## E1 — is a fixed-variance vSAE the same thing as TopK + an L2 penalty?

CLAUDE.md landmine 1 says yes, as algebra: at `sigma = 1` the KL reduces to
`0.5*||mu||^2`, which is exactly what `activation_penalty` computes. That algebra
was verified directly — both trainers return `511.895264` on the same batch, to six
decimals. The empirical question is whether the trained *checkpoints* match.

Two confounds were removed in sequence. Their effect on the measured difference:

| metric | kl_warmup unmatched | + bias form unmatched | **both matched** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `frac_variance_explained` | −58.9 | −50.2 | **−6.6** |
| `frac_recovered` | −43.8 | −39.0 | **−9.7** |
| `frac_alive` | +37.1 | +6.2 | **0.0** |

**`frac_alive` is now exactly identical** — 1.000000 ± 0.0000 in both arms, p = 1.0.
The pilot's headline E1 difference was, on that construct, entirely an artifact of
two implementations being compared as though they were two loss functions.

### But the liveness metric says the arms are still not the same

The pre-registered live-feature metric is the sparsity-relative one (F8b), reported
at **both** thresholds, with the rule fixed in advance: *robust only if both agree;
a disagreement is itself the finding.* Here they disagree — **in direction**:

| threshold | `e1_penalty` | `e1_vsae_ref` | d |
|---|---|---|---|
| < 0.1 × (k/d) | 0.0007 ± 0.0003 | 0.0018 ± 0.0006 | **−2.5** |
| < 0.5 × (k/d) | 0.1846 ± 0.0109 | 0.0928 ± 0.0022 | **+14.0** |

At the tighter cut the vSAE has *more* near-dead features; at the looser cut it has
*half as many*. A pure location shift cannot do that. **The two arms' feature-usage
distributions differ in shape, not merely in position** — which is exactly the case
the two-threshold pre-registration exists to expose, and which a single threshold
would have reported as a clean one-directional result in whichever direction that
threshold happened to favour.

### Verdict on E1, stated carefully

* **As algebra: confirmed.** The objectives are numerically identical.
* **As a claim about checkpoints: not established.** Reconstruction still differs by
  ~0.5% (d ≈ 7–10) and the usage distribution differs in shape (d = 14 at 0.5×).
* **A third unmatched difference is already identified:** `threshold_start_step` is
  500 for `e1_penalty` and 1000 for `e1_vsae_ref`. (`warmup_steps` reads as
  `None` vs `200` in the saved configs but is *not* a confound — `train_topk.py`
  resolves `None` to `max(200, 0.02*10000) = 200`, the same value.)
* The arms also come from two different training scripts, so trainer-internal
  differences beyond the config surface are still possible.

**The equivalence-margin problem is now the binding issue, not statistical power.**
Across-seed SDs are ~0.0008, so a difference of 0.0055 FVE lands at d ≈ 7 and reads
as "significant" at the floor. E1 is an *equivalence* claim and `PROJECT.md` still
lists its margin as an open decision. Without a margin in scientific units, this
test will reject equivalence for differences far too small to matter — and the
margin cannot be chosen now that these numbers have been seen.

## E3 — the ReLU is not a nuisance, it is a large effect

`vsae_topk.py` applies `F.relu(mu)`; the masked-KL trainer did not; the preprint's
equations show none. Rather than declare either correct, both were run (F9b).

| threshold | `e3_masked_kl` (no ReLU, preprint) | `e3_masked_kl_relu` (ReLU, released code) | d |
|---|---|---|---|
| < 0.1 × (k/d) | 0.1309 ± 0.0060 | 0.0298 ± 0.0038 | +20.8 |
| < 0.5 × (k/d) | 0.4654 ± 0.0060 | 0.3134 ± 0.0093 | +19.8 |

Both thresholds agree in direction and magnitude, so this result **is** robust under
the pre-registered rule. Adding the ReLU cuts the near-dead population by a factor
of ~4.4 at the tight threshold.

**This retrospectively justifies not resolving the preprint/code disagreement by
fiat.** Had one trainer simply been patched to match the other, every E3 number
would have silently inherited a d ≈ 20 effect attributed to the KL mask. The
preprint's equations and the released code describe materially different models,
and any claim about "the masked-KL vSAE" must say which one it means.

## E2 — it is the sampling, not the KL

### Stage 1: no beta in the grid gives a working variational SAE

One seed per beta at `var_flag=1`. Baseline mean FVE 0.900335; the rule wanted the
largest beta within 0.02 of it.

| beta | 1e-4 | 1e-3 | 1e-2 | 1e-1 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FVE | 0.4581 | 0.2843 | 0.1367 | 0.0004 | 0.0001 |
| qualifies | no | no | no | no | no |

Nothing qualified; the rule took its fallback and selected 1e-4. The column of noes
is the real output: E2 was designed on the hypothesis that beta = 1.0 was simply too
large once sampling was on, and that a smaller beta would recover a healthy model.
It does not.

### The control: beta = 0 with sampling still on

The pilot cannot distinguish "the KL destroys reconstruction" from "the
reparameterisation does". `kl_coeff = 0, var_flag = 1` is the one configuration that
separates them — sampling on, KL contributing exactly zero. Six seeds.

| config | FVE |
|---|---|
| baseline (deterministic) | 0.900335 ± 0.0004 |
| `var_flag=1`, beta=1e-4 (KL on) | 0.459302 ± 0.0026 |
| `var_flag=1`, **beta=0 (KL off)** | 0.482791 ± 0.0025 |

    total gap to baseline        0.4410
    recovered by removing the KL 0.0235   ( 5.3% of the gap)
    attributable to the SAMPLING 0.4175   (94.7% of the gap)

**Turning the KL entirely off recovers 5.3% of the damage.** The remaining 94.7% is
the reparameterisation. Liveness agrees and is robust across both thresholds:
baseline vs beta=0 sampling is d = +144.9 (0.1×) and +56.9 (0.5×), while beta=1e-4
vs beta=0 is only d = +7.5 and +3.7.

This is a **claim about the architecture, not about a hyperparameter**, and it is
considerably stronger than the beta-tuning story E2 was originally built on. It also
means the degeneracy narrative and the collapse narrative are about different
things: the fixed-variance vSAE (E1) is a deterministic model and reconstructs fine;
the moment sampling is switched on, reconstruction halves regardless of beta.

### Caveats that stand

* `e2_confirm` characterises a model at FVE ≈ 0.46, not a healthy one. Liveness on
  it carries a reconstruction-collapse caveat and belongs in
  `confounders_uncontrolled`, as recorded **before** these runs were read.
* `e2_sampling_only` is **not pre-registered** — proposed and run after stage 1 was
  seen. It is a control rather than a selection step, so it does not contaminate
  `e2_confirm`, but it is exploratory and is reported as such.
* `e2_learned_var` (beta=1.0) remains the cautionary case: FVE 0.000076,
  `frac_recovered` −0.736, and yet `frac_alive` = 1.0000 and `features_used` =
  2047.8/2048. A destroyed model reads as fully alive on the metrics the preprint
  used. The sparsity-relative metric catches it (0.998 near-dead at 0.1×).

# Addendum — 13 seeds, 5 sigma, and E1 resolved

Everything above was measured at 6 seeds/arm, where every p-value sat at 0.00216.
That was the **design floor**, not the evidence running out: the seed permutation
test's attainable p is combinatorial, `2/C(2n,n)`, so 6 seeds cannot exceed 3.07
sigma however large the effect. The battery was rerun at **13 seeds/arm** — the
first n reaching 5 sigma — across 8 arms, 104 checkpoints, 0 failures.

A second ceiling had to be removed first. At 13v13, `C(26,13)` = 10.4M exceeds the
exact-enumeration limit, so the test falls back to Monte Carlo, whose floor is
`1/(n_perm+1)`. The 100k default caps evidence at 1e-5 = **4.42 sigma regardless of
effect size**, and the MC branch was a scalar Python loop, so raising `n_perm` was
impractical. Vectorising it made 2M draws take 0.5 s (from minutes). Results below
use `n_perm = 4,000,000`, floor 2.5e-07 = 5.16 sigma.

## E1 is confirmed on the pre-registered construct

**Correction to the body of this document.** It named `threshold_start_step` as
E1's remaining unmatched difference. That was wrong — the threshold is only read
via `encode(use_threshold=True)`, which only `batch_top_k.py` calls, so the field
is inert here. The real difference was **initial decoder scale**: `top_k.py` uses
`set_decoder_norm_to_unit_norm` (norm 1.0), `vsae_topk.py` used 0.1.

Run as a factor rather than silently matched, on the **pre-registered liveness
metric**, at 13 seeds:

| comparison | < 0.1x k/d | < 0.5x k/d | thresholds | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E1a** TopK+L2 vs vSAE (init 0.1) | d = −2.8, 5.03σ | d = +13.0, 5.03σ | **disagree in direction** | not robust |
| **E1b** TopK+L2 vs vSAE (init 1.0) | d = **+0.8, p = 0.114** | d = **+0.2, p = 0.614** | **agree** | **indistinguishable** |
| **E1c** init factor alone | d = +2.8, 5.03σ | d = −18.0, 5.03σ | disagree in direction | shape change |

**With decoder init matched, `e1_penalty` and `e1_vsae_ref_unitinit` cannot be
told apart on the pre-registered live-feature metric** — d = 0.8 and 0.2, neither
significant, both thresholds agreeing, at the highest power run. The degeneracy
claim of CLAUDE.md landmine 1 is **confirmed on the construct E1 was built to
test**, and the entire apparent difference in the pilot was three stacked
implementation mismatches: KL warmup, bias parameterisation, decoder init scale.

E1c shows why the two-threshold pre-registration earned its keep a second time:
the init changes the **shape** of the usage distribution (direction flips between
thresholds), not its location. A single threshold would have reported a clean
one-directional effect in whichever direction it happened to favour.

## The reconstruction metrics point the other way, and that is unresolved

On FVE and `frac_recovered`, matching the init makes the arms **more** different:

| comparison | FVE diff | d | sigma |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1a (init 0.1) | −0.0059 | −5.7 | 5.03 |
| E1b (init 1.0, matched) | +0.0181 | +16.5 | 5.16 |

So the historical 0.1 init was *closer* to `e1_penalty` on reconstruction while
being *further* on liveness. The two constructs disagree, and only liveness is
pre-registered.

**The leading candidate is a genuine asymmetry, not a config value.**
`vsae_topk.py` imports `remove_gradient_parallel_to_decoder_directions` and
**never calls it** (grep count: 0); `top_k.py` calls it every step, before
renormalising the decoder to unit norm. Renormalising without projecting out the
parallel gradient means the optimiser fights the constraint — the radial component
is applied and then immediately undone — which is a per-feature change in
effective learning rate. This looks like an oversight rather than a design choice,
and it is the next thing to test as a factor.

## E3 and E2 at 5 sigma

Both robust: thresholds agree in direction and magnitude.

| test | < 0.1x k/d | < 0.5x k/d |
|---|---|---|
| E3 no-ReLU vs ReLU | d = +19.3, 5.03σ | d = +15.3, 5.03σ |
| E2 baseline vs beta=0 sampling | d = +118.2, 5.03σ | d = +62.1, 5.03σ |
| E2 beta=1e-4 vs beta=0 | d = +6.9, 5.03σ | d = +3.9, 5.03σ |

E2's attribution is unchanged at 13 seeds: baseline FVE 0.900159 ± 0.0006,
beta=1e-4 0.460894 ± 0.0025, beta=0 0.484106 ± 0.0022 — removing the KL entirely
recovers 5.3% of the gap, and **94.7% is the reparameterisation**.

## The equivalence-margin problem, now unavoidable

E1b is indistinguishable on liveness but clears **5.16 sigma** on a reconstruction
difference of 0.0181, and E1a clears 5.03 sigma on 0.0059. More power made E1
*more* definitively "different" on gaps of half a percent. This is exactly what an
equivalence claim without a margin does, and no amount of compute fixes it: the
margin must be a statement about what difference would **matter**, set
independently of these numbers.

# Addendum 2 — the gradient projection, and the two constructs part company

The previous addendum named the decoder-gradient projection as the leading
candidate for E1's remaining reconstruction gap: `top_k.py` calls
`remove_gradient_parallel_to_decoder_directions` every step, `vsae_topk.py` imports
it and never calls it, and both renormalise the decoder to unit norm — so the
radial component of the decoder gradient is applied by the optimiser and
immediately undone by the constraint. Run as a factor
(`project_decoder_grad`, default off; arm `e1_vsae_ref_gradproj` =
`e1_vsae_ref_unitinit` + the projection), 13 seeds, `n_perm = 4e6`.

**It was the right candidate for reconstruction, and it breaks the liveness
result.**

## The factor's own effect

| metric | unitinit | gradproj | d | sigma |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FVE | 0.828232 ± 0.0011 | 0.842513 ± 0.0009 | −14.3 | 5.03 |
| `frac_recovered` | 0.918932 ± 0.0006 | 0.926509 ± 0.0004 | −15.3 | 5.16* |
| < 0.1x k/d | 0.000451 ± 0.0004 | 0.003042 ± 0.0017 | −2.4 | 4.48 |
| < 0.5x k/d | 0.181603 ± 0.0074 | 0.219651 ± 0.0078 | −5.0 | 5.03 |

The projection **improves reconstruction and costs liveness**, both thresholds
agreeing in direction. It is not a small correction: it is the largest single
factor found in E1 so far on reconstruction.

## E1 with the projection matched

| metric | `e1_penalty` | `e1_vsae_ref_gradproj` | diff | d | sigma |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FVE | 0.846335 ± 0.0011 | 0.842513 ± 0.0009 | +0.0038 | +3.8 | 5.16* |
| `frac_recovered` | 0.929044 ± 0.0006 | 0.926509 ± 0.0004 | +0.0025 | +4.9 | 5.03 |
| < 0.1x k/d | 0.000714 ± 0.0003 | 0.003042 ± 0.0017 | −0.0023 | −2.4 | 4.30 |
| < 0.5x k/d | 0.183556 ± 0.0115 | 0.219651 ± 0.0078 | −0.0361 | −3.7 | 5.03 |

`*` = p is at the binding floor; sigma is a lower bound there, not a measurement.

**Reconstruction: 78.9% of the FVE gap closes** (0.0181 → 0.0038) and 74.9% of the
`frac_recovered` gap (0.0101 → 0.0025). The hypothesis is confirmed — the
unprojected gradient was most of E1's reconstruction residual.

**Liveness: the pre-registered claim now fails.** On `e1_vsae_ref_unitinit` the
arms were indistinguishable (d = +0.8, p = 0.114; d = +0.2, p = 0.614). On
`e1_vsae_ref_gradproj` they separate at d = −2.4 and −3.7, **both thresholds
agreeing in direction**, which by F8b makes it a robust effect rather than the
shape change the two-threshold rule caught twice before.

## The part that is not just bookkeeping

The projection moved the vSAE's liveness **away** from `e1_penalty`, not toward it.
`e1_penalty` runs `top_k.py`, which has had the projection all along:

| | < 0.5x k/d |
|---|---|
| `e1_penalty` (TopK+L2, projection on) | 0.183556 |
| vSAE, projection **off** | 0.181603 |
| vSAE, projection **on** | 0.219651 |

Adding to the vSAE the exact update rule the TopK arm already used made it *less*
like the TopK arm on the pre-registered metric. The same optimiser-side change has
opposite-signed effects in the two implementations, so this is an interaction, not
a missing match.

That matters for what E1 can claim. The degeneracy is an identity between two
*objectives*: at sigma = 1 the vSAE's KL is `0.5*||mu||^2`, verified to six decimals
on a shared batch (`511.895264`). Nothing in it says two optimisers descending that
same objective arrive at the same place. What these three arms measure is that they
do not, and that which construct you score decides the verdict:

* on **reconstruction**, matching the update rule makes the arms nearly agree;
* on **liveness** — the pre-registered construct — matching the update rule makes
  them disagree.

## A mechanism candidate, not yet tested

The two trainers define "fired" differently for dead-feature tracking, which is the
only part of the loss that targets liveness:

* `top_k.py:543` — `did_fire[top_indices_BK.flatten()] = True`: **selected by
  TopK**, whatever its value.
* `vsae_topk.py:888` — `active_features = (sparse_features_BF.sum(0) > 0)`:
  selected **and strictly positive**.

Both encoders apply a ReLU before selection (`top_k.py:183`, `vsae_topk.py:290`),
so values are non-negative and the two rules differ on exactly one case: a feature
TopK selects at value 0, which resets the counter in `top_k.py` and does not in
`vsae_topk.py`. The vSAE therefore declares features dead sooner, which changes how
much AuxK pressure they get. Whether that case is common enough to explain a d of
−3.7 is unmeasured. It is the obvious next factor, and it is also the point at
which the strategy of matching one asymmetry at a time needs a stopping rule —
see `PROJECT.md`, open decisions.

# Addendum 3 — the learned sigma, read at last

`PROJECT.md` specified this diagnostic when E2 was designed and it was never run:
*if the learned sigma collapses toward 0, the degeneracy is the optimum rather than
an implementation accident, which is a substantially stronger result.* The
`var_encoder` weights have been on disk since the arms were trained.

Measured with `falsification/read_learned_sigma.py` over 20,000 gelu-1l
activations, the same batch for every checkpoint, 13 seeds per arm.

## The posterior collapses completely, and then keeps going

| arm | beta | mean raw `log_var` | max over 41M values | fraction at/below the clamp floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `e2_confirm` | 1e-4 | **−66.92** ± 0.06 | −31.5 | **1.0000** |
| `e2_sampling_only` | 0 | **−71.32** ± 0.07 | −30.5 | **1.0000** |

`log_var` is clamped to [−6, 2] in both `reparameterize` (`vsae_topk.py:333`) and
`_compute_kl_loss` (`:856`). Every one of 41 million measured values — every
feature, every token, every seed, both arms — is **at or below that floor**, and the
single largest is 24 log-units beneath it. The encoder is asking for
sigma ≈ 3e−15 and the clamp gives it exp(−3) = 0.0498.

**The variational SAE's optimum is the deterministic SAE.** The preprint's
`_fixed_var` checkpoints were not a run-script accident; they are where training
goes when you let it choose. This is the strongest form the E1 degeneracy claim
could take, and it needed no new training.

The KL's direction confirms the reading. For `N(mu, sigma^2)` against `N(0, I)` the
KL is minimised in sigma at **sigma = 1**, so it pulls sigma *up* while
reconstruction pulls it down. `e2_confirm` (beta = 1e-4) sits 4.4 log-units above
`e2_sampling_only` (beta = 0) — the pull is real, in the predicted direction, and
utterly overwhelmed.

## The noise at convergence is harmless, so the damage is in the weights

`evaluation.py:51` calls `dictionary(x, output_features=True)` without passing
`training=`, and `VSAETopK.forward` defaults it to `True` — so **every recorded FVE
for a `var_flag=1` arm was measured with the reparameterisation active**. That
raised an obvious candidate: perhaps E2's damage is just noise injected at
evaluation time.

It is not. On identical data, with sampling on versus off:

| arm | FVE sampled | FVE deterministic | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| `e2_confirm` | 0.456461 ± 0.0007 | 0.456472 ± 0.0007 | **0.000012** |
| `e2_sampling_only` | 0.479169 ± 0.0007 | 0.479179 ± 0.0007 | **0.000010** |

Turning the sampling off changes FVE by one part in forty thousand. At the collapsed
sigma the median noise-to-signal ratio on selected features is 0.0129 and no
selection is noise-dominated, so the forward pass is effectively deterministic
already.

**So the reparameterisation's cost is an optimisation-path effect, not an
inference-time one.** The model is not being damaged by noise at convergence — it
has already learned to switch the noise off as hard as the clamp permits, and it is
*still* at FVE 0.46 against baseline's 0.90. Training under sampling lands the
optimiser in a substantially worse solution and it does not recover after the
posterior collapses.

This sharpens E2 rather than overturning it. "94.7% of the damage is the
reparameterisation" stands, and now has a mechanism: the harm is done on the way,
not at the destination. Sigma starts at exp(−1) = 0.368 by default
(`log_var_init = −2.0`) when mu is still small, so the noise-to-signal ratio is
worst exactly when the TopK selection is being established.

**Prediction, untested:** initialising `log_var` far below the clamp floor, or
annealing sigma toward zero over the first steps, should recover most of the gap.
If it does, the reparameterisation is not intrinsically incompatible with TopK — a
bad initial noise scale is. If it does not, the incompatibility is real.

## One sub-hypothesis falsified in passing

Addendum 2 and the "claims worth opening" list proposed that TopK might be forced
to select features whose `mu` is exactly 0 after the ReLU, making the noise the
entire signal for those selections. **It does not happen.** With k = 256 of
d = 2048 there are 975 (`e2_confirm`) to 1064 (`e2_sampling_only`) strictly
positive features per token, four times what TopK needs; the measured fraction of
selected features with `mu == 0` is exactly 0.

A related caution for the selection-instability test proposed as claim 3: measuring
Jaccard overlap of selected indices on *converged* checkpoints will show almost no
instability — the FVE gap above proves the whole forward pass is stable at the
collapsed sigma. The instability that matters, if it matters, is **during early
training**, and must be measured there.

## The dead-feature tracking factor is a no-op — do not run it

Addendum 2 identified the two trainers' differing definitions of "fired" as the
next factor to run, on the reasoning that a feature TopK selects at value 0 resets
the counter in `top_k.py` and not in `vsae_topk.py`. The selection statistics above
kill it, measured on the E1 arms themselves rather than assumed from the E2 ones:

| arm | strictly positive `mu` per token | selected features with `mu == 0` |
|---|---|---|
| `e1_vsae_ref_unitinit` | 458.7 ± 0.6 | **0.0000** |
| `e1_vsae_ref_gradproj` | 455.4 ± 0.6 | **0.0000** |

With 455 positive features available and k = 256 needed, TopK is never forced to
pad its selection with zeros, so the two rules cannot disagree. The factor would
have cost 30 GPU-minutes and 13 more checkpoints to measure a difference that
cannot occur.

The one caveat: this is measured at convergence. The two rules would begin to
diverge only if the positive count fell below k during training, and it moves from
roughly 1024 at initialisation (half of 2048 surviving the ReLU) to 455 at the end
— it does not approach 256. Treat the factor as closed unless a training-time
instrument shows otherwise.

# Addendum 4 — the E1 code diff, enumerated and frozen

`PROJECT.md` "Next steps" #2 step 1: *enumerate every difference between the two
E1 arms' code paths by reading the files, and freeze the list before running
anything.* Step 1 before step 2 is what makes the remaining arms confirmatory
rather than exploratory, and what dissolves E1's forking-path problem — the
factor set is now fixed by a code diff rather than by which arms happened to
disagree.

**Scope.** The arms are `e1_penalty` (`train_topk.py` →
`top_k_with_feature_penalty.py`) and `e1_vsae_ref_*` (`train_vsae_topk.py` →
`vsae_topk.py`). `top_k_with_feature_penalty.py` is `top_k.py` plus the
`activation_penalty` term and nothing else — verified by diff, 2 hunks, both the
penalty. Everything below therefore reads as a `top_k.py` ↔ `vsae_topk.py` diff,
plus the two training scripts and the shared `trainSAE` loop.

**The config surface is fully matched.** Every field both arms expose is equal in
the saved `config.json`s: `dict_size` 2048, `k` 256, `lr` 8e-4, `warmup_steps`
200, `decay_start` 8000, `sparsity_warmup_steps` 500, `auxk_alpha` 1/32,
`dead_feature_threshold` 1000, `gradient_clip_norm` 1.0, `dtype` bfloat16. Both
scripts build the same `TransformerLensActivationBuffer` (same dataset, hook,
`n_ctxs`, `ctx_len`, batch sizes) and call `trainSAE` with the same
`normalize_activations=True` and `autocast_dtype`. So every difference that
remains is in code, and this is the complete list of them.

## Item 15, found while implementing item 7

`set_decoder_norm_to_unit_norm` divides by `norm + eps` with
`eps = finfo(dtype).eps`, and in bfloat16 that is 0.0078 against an initial column
norm of ~0.29. So **`top_k.py`'s decoder does not start at unit norm at all — it
starts at 0.9737**, while `vsae_topk.py` normalises exactly and starts at 1.0001.
`decoder_init_scale=1.0` matches the two arms nominally and leaves 2.7% of scale
unmatched in fact. (Both then converge to the same fixed point of
`n -> n/(n + eps)`, i.e. 1 − eps = 0.9922, and stay there, since both renormalise
with the same helper every step once `use_april_update_mode=False`.)

The asymmetry therefore survives exactly one optimiser step — and that step runs
at **learning rate exactly zero**, because `warmup_steps = 200` and
`get_lr_schedule` returns `step / warmup_steps` at `step = 0`. It reaches the
trajectory only through Adam's moment estimates. That is why it is filed as a
no-op rather than as a factor.

It is worth writing down anyway, because it is a good example of the thing this
whole enumeration exists to catch: `decoder_init_scale` was introduced to close a
**10x** init-scale gap and was described as matching the two arms' initial decoder
scale. It matched it to 2.7%, and nothing in either config would ever have said so.
The `e1_vsae_ref_fullmatch` arm closes it exactly, because `decoder_init_dist=
"uniform"` reproduces `top_k.py`'s draw through the same helper, eps and all —
which is also why that draw has to use the real `1/sqrt(fan_in)` bound rather than
a convenient `(-1, 1)`: the eps offset depends on the absolute scale of the draw,
so the wrong bound would land at 0.9994 and quietly reintroduce a scale difference
while claiming to remove one.

## Item 6, settled by measurement rather than by a run

The two arms penalise the same tensor with the same coefficient but not with the
same function:

```
top_k_with_feature_penalty.py:570   activation_penalty * 0.5 * sum(f^2)
vsae_topk.py:867                    kl_coeff       * 0.5 * sum(clamp(z, -10, 10)^2)
```

`f` and `z` are the same quantity at `var_flag=0` — `relu(encoder(x - b))`, the
full pre-TopK vector, unselected features included. The objectives coincide
exactly below the clamp and diverge above it, where the vSAE's penalty goes flat
and **its gradient becomes exactly zero**: a feature the TopK+L2 arm is still
being pushed down on is left alone by the vSAE.

This is the only enumerated factor that touches the *objective* rather than the
optimiser, so it is the only one that could qualify the six-decimal loss identity
E1 rests on — that identity was checked on one batch, and it holds only where the
clamp is idle. Measured with `falsification/read_penalty_clamp.py` over 20,000
activations, the same batch for every checkpoint, 13 seeds per arm, reported in
**training space** (see below):

| arm | max pre-TopK activation, over 13 seeds | fraction above the clamp | penalty mass lost | gradient lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `e1_penalty` | 0.1865 | **0** | 0 | 0 |
| `e1_vsae_ref_unitinit` | 0.1944 | **0** | 0 | 0 |
| `e1_vsae_ref_gradproj` | 0.1840 | **0** | 0 | 0 |

The largest activation anywhere is **0.194 against a clamp at 10** — a factor of
51 of headroom, in every arm and every seed. On this data the two penalties are
the same function, and the loss identity is unqualified. Do not spend a run on it.

Scale is the one place this is easy to get wrong. Training runs on activations
normalised to unit mean squared norm and the clamp acts in that space, but
checkpoints are saved with their biases scaled back up by `norm_factor`, so a
saved model applied to raw activations returns `norm_factor * mu_train`. The
numbers above are divided by `norm_factor`, re-estimated as 25.54 with the same
estimator the trainer used. Reading them in the saved space would have put the
maximum at 4.96 and made the headroom look like a factor of 2.

**Caveat, the same one item 5 carries:** measured at convergence. At
initialisation the encoder rows have unit norm and `||x_train|| ≈ 1`, so `mu ≲ 1`,
and the penalty pushes it down from there — the clamp would have to be reached by
the encoder growing ~50x against its own penalty. Treat item 6 as closed unless a
training-time instrument shows otherwise.

## What the enumeration changes

Before it, "which asymmetry do we match next?" had no principled answer and each
new one flipped a verdict — which is the forking-path problem recorded under
**Open decisions**. After it, the answer is *there is one left, and it is item 7*.
That makes the stopping rule pre-registrable in the form PROJECT.md asked for:
**run item 7, then stop, because the diff is empty.**

Item 7 is also the weakest candidate on the list. Both inits produce unit-norm
decoder columns at `decoder_init_scale=1.0`; they differ only in the direction
distribution (normalised i.i.d. uniform is not exactly rotationally symmetric,
normalised i.i.d. Gaussian is), and in `d = 512` that difference is small. It is
a ~30-minute arm, and the honest expectation is that it moves nothing — which is
worth having, because "the decomposition is complete and the last factor is null"
is a much stronger closing statement than "we stopped looking".

## A shared defect found in passing, not an asymmetry

`norm_factor` is recorded in **neither** arm's `config.json`. `trainSAE` does
`trainer.config["norm_factor"] = norm_factor` (`training.py:212`), but
`TopKTrainer.config` and `VSAETopKTrainer.config` are both `@property` — each
call builds a fresh dict, so the write lands on a temporary and is discarded. It
affects both arms identically and so confounds nothing, but it means the input
scale a checkpoint was trained at is not recoverable from the checkpoint, and any
later analysis in training space has to re-estimate it as this one did.

## What this licenses, and what it does not

**It licenses the decomposition claim**, which is what PROJECT.md "Next steps" #2
proposed and is stronger than the equivalence verdict E1 was originally posed as:

> Two implementations of an algebraically identical objective differ at d ≈ 16 on
> reconstruction and d ≈ 13 on feature liveness. The entire difference decomposes
> into four optimiser- and initialisation-side details — a KL warmup schedule, a
> bias parameterisation, an initial decoder scale, a gradient projection, and an
> initial weight distribution — **none of which appears in either paper's
> equations.** With all of them matched the two implementations become
> statistically indistinguishable at 13 seeds.

**It does not by itself license "the two are equivalent."** This is a failure to
detect a difference, not a demonstration of its absence, and converting it into a
formal equivalence verdict still needs the margin under PROJECT.md **Open
decisions (b)** — what difference in live fraction would *matter*. What has
changed is that the margin is no longer load-bearing for the *direction* of the
verdict: the observed differences are 0.0003 FVE and 0.006 in live fraction at
0.5x, against arms that differed by 0.018 and 0.092 before the factors were
matched. The margin now decides how strongly to state a null, not which way the
result goes.

**The stopping rule held.** The factor set was frozen by a code diff before this
arm was run, the arm was specified from that list rather than chosen because it
might close the gap, and its prediction was recorded and falsified. The list is
now empty, so E1 stops here — not because the arms finally agree, which is exactly
the forking path the enumeration was built to avoid.

# Addendum 6 — the liveness–reconstruction frontier

PROJECT.md "Claims worth opening" #2, run 2026-09-04 with
`falsification/frontier.py`. Figure: `workshop/figs/frontier.pdf`.

**Exploratory.** Not part of the pre-registered battery; every arm in it was
trained for another purpose and no hypothesis about the shape of this
relationship was recorded before the points were plotted. Nothing here carries a
confirmatory p-value.

Two measurement choices worth stating before the result. The liveness axis is the
**near-dead fraction**, not `frac_alive` — `frac_alive` is 1.000000 in every arm
including the fully collapsed ones, so it has no variance to correlate — and both
pre-registered thresholds are reported. The unit is the **arm**, not the
checkpoint: 13 seeds of one arm are 13 draws of one architecture, not 13 draws of
"an architecture", so a correlation over 136 checkpoints is pseudo-replicated
(CLAUDE.md, unit of analysis). Checkpoint-level numbers are printed and labelled;
the arm-mean ones are the ones quoted.

## Pooled, the two metrics look unrelated

Across all 11 replicated arms:

| threshold | arm-mean Spearman rho | p |
|---|---|---|
| `< 0.1x k/d` | −0.136 | 0.69 |
| `< 0.5x k/d` | −0.136 | 0.69 |

Read alone, this licenses treating reconstruction and liveness as two independent
pieces of evidence. **It is an artifact of averaging two opposite-signed
relationships.**

## The sign reverses, and no chosen threshold is doing the work

A "healthy vs collapsed" cut picked after seeing these points would carry the
result, so every possible cut between adjacent arms is scanned instead:

| cut on FVE | n below | rho below | n above | rho above |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.656 | 3 | −1.000 | 8 | +0.857 |
| 0.835 | 4 | −1.000 | 7 | +0.786 |
| 0.844 | 5 | −0.900 | 6 | +1.000 |
| 0.847 | 6 | −0.829 | 5 | +1.000 |
| 0.850 | 7 | −0.750 | 4 | +1.000 |
| 0.859 | 8 | −0.667 | 3 | +1.000 |

(`< 0.1x`; the `< 0.5x` scan has the same signs at every row.) **Below the cut the
correlation is negative at every cut; above it, positive at every cut, at both
thresholds.** The reversal is a property of the data, not of a threshold.

## Among working models, the frontier slopes the other way

The eight arms above the gap — every arm anyone would call a working SAE:

| threshold | arm-mean Spearman rho | p |
|---|---|---|
| `< 0.1x k/d` | **+0.857** | 0.0065 |
| `< 0.5x k/d` | **+0.690** | 0.058 |

**Better reconstruction buys MORE near-dead features**, not fewer. Both
thresholds agree in direction. Mechanically this is unsurprising once stated: the
baseline has no penalty and reconstructs best (FVE 0.900) while leaving 75% of its
dictionary near-dead; the penalised arms spread usage four-fold and pay ~0.05 FVE
for it. The frontier is largely the **usage-spreading pressure** axis.

That is the point, not a weakness. A nuisance variable that neither metric names
moves both of them, which is Failure 2 of the thesis — measured on our own
battery rather than argued in the abstract.

## What this does to "A reconstructs better AND has fewer dead features"

Among working models the two metrics are close to one degree of freedom (rho
0.86): knowing where an arm sits on the frontier substantially determines both.
Reporting them as independent evidence for one conclusion overstates the evidence.

The direction sharpens this into a usable diagnostic. Because the frontier slopes
**up**, a claim that architecture A both reconstructs better *and* has fewer
near-dead features is not doubly supported — it asserts that A is **off** the
frontier, which is a much stronger and much rarer claim than either half. It
should be argued for as such, and it is exactly the shape of claim the preprint
made.

## Off-frontier arms exist, and the one we have is an implementation artifact

On a genuine frontier every pair trades. Counting pairs where one arm is better on
FVE *and* on both liveness thresholds:

| set | dominated pairs | trading pairs |
|---|---|---|
| all 11 replicated arms | 27 of 55 (49%) | 28 |
| **the 8 working models** | **3 of 28 (11%)** | **25** |

Among working models almost everything trades — the frontier is real. All three
exceptions are the same arm being dominated: `e1_vsae_ref_gradproj` is beaten
outright by `e1_penalty`, `e1_vsae_ref` and `e1_vsae_ref_fullmatch`. That is the
**half-matched** E1 implementation — the gradient projection matched but not the
init draw — and addendum 5 already showed matching the last factor moves it back
onto the frontier, improving reconstruction *and* liveness at once. Being off the
frontier here is an implementation artifact, not an architectural property.

## Optimiser details do not move you along the frontier

The five arms with an algebraically identical objective, differing only in the
details E1 enumerated:

| threshold | arm-mean Spearman rho | p |
|---|---|---|
| `< 0.1x k/d` | +0.400 | 0.51 |
| `< 0.5x k/d` | −0.300 | 0.62 |

Neither significant, and **the two thresholds disagree in sign** — the disagreement
the pre-registration exists to expose (F8b). Within a fixed objective the arms
scatter rather than tracing a curve. PROJECT.md speculated that `gradproj` "bought
reconstruction and paid in liveness, which is exactly what movement along a
frontier looks like". With the full ladder in hand that reading is wrong:
`fullmatch` bought **both**, and `gradproj` is simply dominated.

So the frontier is a property of what the objective asks for, and implementation
details knock arms off it rather than sliding them along it.

## Caveats

* Eight arms is a small n, and they were not designed to span a frontier — they
  are the battery's arms, trained for other questions.
* `p = 0.058` at the loose threshold is not significant on its own. The claim
  rests on the two thresholds agreeing in direction and on the cut scan, not on
  either p-value.
* The correlation is substantially the penalty axis. It is evidence that *these*
  metrics co-vary with *this* nuisance in *this* battery, not a general law about
  SAEs.

# Addendum 7 — the sigma-annealing arm: 84% of E2's gap is the initial noise scale, not the reparameterisation itself

Addendum 3 found the learned sigma collapses completely under `e2_sampling_only`
(mean raw `log_var` −71.3 against a clamp floor of −6.0) and that the damage is an
optimisation-path effect: turning sampling off at convergence changes FVE by one
part in forty thousand, so the model has already learned to suppress the noise as
hard as it can and is still stuck at FVE 0.48. The untested prediction recorded
there: sigma starts at `exp(-1) = 0.368` (`log_var_init = -2.0`) while `mu` is
still small, so the noise-to-signal ratio is worst exactly when TopK selection is
being established — initialise `log_var` at or below the clamp floor and most of
the gap should recover if a bad initial noise scale is the whole story.

`log_var_init` is now a factor on `VSAETopKConfig` and `ExperimentConfig`
(`training_scripts/train_vsae_topk.py`), the same way `decoder_init_dist` was
added for E1. `e2_sigma_low_init` is `e2_sampling_only` (`var_flag=1`,
`kl_coeff=0.0`) with `log_var_init=-8.0` — below the `[-6, 2]` clamp in
`reparameterize`, so sigma starts at exactly `exp(-3) = 0.0498` instead of
declining there over training. 13 seeds, `falsification/compare_arms.py`:

| metric | `e2_sampling_only` | `e2_sigma_low_init` | `baseline` | d (vs sampling_only) | sigma |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FVE | 0.4841 ± 0.0022 | **0.8338 ± 0.0007** | 0.9002 ± 0.0006 | −240.9 | 5.16* |
| `frac_recovered` | 0.6019 ± 0.0021 | **0.9249 ± 0.0004** | 0.9643 ± 0.0005 | −258.7 | 5.03 |
| `frac_alive` | 0.8012 ± 0.0165 | **1.0000 ± 0.0000** | 0.9934 ± 0.0025 | −24.1 | 5.03 |
| `< 0.1x k/d` | 0.3807 ± 0.0050 | **0.0019 ± 0.0012** | 0.7542 ± 0.0013 | +122.7 | 5.03 |
| `< 0.5x k/d` | 0.4615 ± 0.0080 | **0.0577 ± 0.0048** | 0.7544 ± 0.0014 | +63.2 | 5.03 |

(* at the design floor for `n_perm=4M` — the true sigma is higher, not lower.)

**Answer: mostly (a), a little (b).** Fixing the initial noise scale alone closes
**84.1%** of the FVE gap between `e2_sampling_only` and `baseline`
((0.8338−0.4841)/(0.9002−0.4841)) and **89.1%** of the `frac_recovered` gap. Both
liveness thresholds agree in direction and both put `e2_sigma_low_init` *more*
even than `baseline` itself, not just recovered toward it — `frac_alive` reaches
**1.0000** (zero dead features, 13/13 seeds) against baseline's 0.9934, and the
near-dead fractions (0.0019, 0.0577) are both roughly 15–70x **smaller** than
baseline's (0.7542, 0.7544). This is not a violation of the addendum-6 frontier —
it slopes the direction the frontier predicts (worse FVE pairs with fewer
near-dead features) — but the magnitude is extreme: this arm has substantially
better liveness than everything else in the battery at a reconstruction cost of
only 0.066 FVE against baseline.

A **real, 5-sigma residual remains**: `e2_sigma_low_init` does not fully reach
`baseline` (d = +103 on FVE, `p` at the design floor). So the reparameterisation is
not *entirely* free once the noise-scale confound is removed — but the effect
shrinks by an order of magnitude, from d ≈ −241 against the naive `log_var_init`
to d ≈ +103 in the other direction against baseline, i.e. what was the dominant
story (94.7% "is the reparameterisation") turns out to be overwhelmingly an
initialisation artifact, not an architectural incompatibility.

**Confirms the mechanism, doesn't just patch around it.**
`read_learned_sigma.py --arm e2_sigma_low_init` shows every one of 13 seeds at
**100.00% of measured values at the clamp floor**, mean raw `log_var` ≈ −206 (far
below −8, the model still pushes toward zero noise, it just starts there instead
of arriving late) and `noise>mu` = 0.0000 throughout — this arm is
deterministic-equivalent from step 0, exactly as designed, and it is the arm that
recovers. That the *only* changed quantity is when in training sigma reaches the
floor, and that changing only that recovers 84–89% of the gap, is direct evidence
for the mechanism proposed in addendum 3: the damage is done while `mu` is small
and the noise-to-signal ratio is worst, not by the reparameterisation as such.

**Revises the E2 headline.** "94.7% of the damage is the reparameterisation, not
the KL" (RESULTS main text) is still true as a statement about *what* breaks
(sampling, not the KL term) but is now known to be substantially an
**initialisation artifact**, not an intrinsic property of combining
reparameterisation with discrete TopK selection. This sharpens rather than
undercuts Claims-worth-opening #3 (discreteness-under-noise): the residual 5-sigma
gap that survives fixing the init is the more interesting quantity to explain now,
and the Jaccard-overlap-during-early-training instrumentation proposed there is
the natural next step to characterise it directly rather than inferring it from an
init sweep.

**Caveat.** This arm, like `e2_sampling_only`, is not pre-registered — it is a
control proposed after E2's stage-1 pilot and the sigma reading were seen. It does
not contaminate `e2_confirm` or `e2_sampling_only`, but it is exploratory and
should be reported as such.

# Addendum 8 — a real bug in `scale_biases`: the posterior never collapsed, and the residual E2 gap IS selection instability

Building the Jaccard-overlap instrumentation planned in Next steps #1 (measuring
TopK selection churn during early training, to test whether it explains
`e2_sigma_low_init`'s residual gap against baseline) turned up something bigger
than what it was built to test: **a genuine bug in `VSAETopK.scale_biases`, present
since `var_flag=1` was added, that corrupts every saved checkpoint's
`var_encoder.bias`** and overturns two of addendum 3's central claims.

## The bug

`trainSAE` calls `scale_biases(norm_factor)` before every checkpoint save (final
and intermediate) to convert a model trained on activations normalised to unit
mean squared norm into one correct on raw activations, then calls
`scale_biases(1/norm_factor)` immediately after to restore the live training
state. This is exactly right for `encoder.bias` and `decoder.bias` (or `self.bias`):
they sit additively on the same axis as `x` and `mu`, and ReLU is
positive-homogeneous, so multiplying them by `norm_factor` makes `mu_raw = norm_factor
* mu_normalised` exactly.

The old code multiplied `var_encoder.bias` by the same factor. This is wrong:
`log_var` parameterises `sigma = exp(0.5*clamp(log_var, -6, 2))`, and clamp+exp are
not scale-homogeneous, so no multiplicative rescaling of the bias reproduces "the
same model, evaluated on activations `norm_factor` times larger." For gelu-1l
layer 0 (`norm_factor` ≈ 25.75, derived exactly from the ratio of a saved bias to
its configured `log_var_init` — e.g. `-51.5 / -2.0`, identical across all 13 seeds
of `e2_confirm` and `e2_sampling_only` and both arms of the new `_early` runs),
`log_var_init=-2.0` gets saved as `-51.5` and `-8.0` as `-206.0`. Reading such a
checkpoint's `log_var` on raw activations (the codebase's standard convention,
used by `evaluate()`, `read_learned_sigma.py`, and this script's own first draft)
therefore reports a fully clamp-saturated posterior for **any** arm, regardless of
what it actually learned.

**Fixed** in `dictionary_learning/trainers/{vsae_topk,vsae_topk_masked_kl,vsae_jump_relu}.py`:
`var_encoder.bias` (`b_enc_var` for the JumpReLU trainer) is left untouched, and
`var_encoder.weight` (`W_enc_var`) is divided by `scale` instead. Since
`log_var = W_var @ x_processed + b_var` is linear (no nonlinearity between the
input and this particular output), `x -> scale*x, W_var -> W_var/scale` exactly
preserves `log_var`'s value — a freshly-trained checkpoint now reads correctly on
raw activations with no special-casing, verified directly: a fresh
`e2_sampling_only` seed's saved `var_encoder.bias` is `-2.0` (not `-51.5`), and
reading its `ae.pt` on raw activations with no correction gives `log_var` mean
`-2.745`, matching the manually-corrected reading of the old-style checkpoints
below to three decimals. `vsae_topk_masked_kl.py` is dormant for this (E3 runs at
`var_flag=0`); `vsae_jump_relu.py` has never been trained with `var_flag=1`; both
were fixed pre-emptively so the bug is not there waiting.

**Every checkpoint already on disk was saved with the old, buggy code** and needs
the same correction applied by hand when read: divide both `var_encoder.bias` and
`var_encoder.weight` by `norm_factor` before calling `encode()` on raw activations.
`read_selection_jaccard.py` does this now; `read_learned_sigma.py`'s addendum-3
numbers below do not, and are corrected here.

## Addendum 3's collapse claim does not survive

Reading `e2_confirm` and `e2_sampling_only`'s final checkpoints correctly (all 13
seeds each, same batch):

| arm | addendum 3's reading (buggy) | corrected reading | frac at/below floor (corrected) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `e2_confirm` | mean log_var −66.92 | **mean log_var ≈ −2.58** (13/13 seeds agree to 2 d.p.) | **0.0000** |
| `e2_sampling_only` | mean log_var −71.32 | **mean log_var ≈ −2.74** (13/13 seeds agree to 2 d.p.) | **0.0000** |

Not one value in either arm sits at or below the clamp floor. **"The variational
SAE's optimum is the deterministic SAE" does not hold** — the encoder settles at a
moderate, non-degenerate `sigma ≈ exp(-1.3) ≈ 0.27`, not the floor's `0.0498`.

`e2_sigma_low_init` is a special case and its own numbers are **unaffected**:
`log_var_init=-8.0` sits below the clamp floor before the bug's multiplication is
even applied, so the buggy and corrected readings coincide (both saturate at
`sigma=0.0498`) — this is why addendum 7's measurements of that arm needed no
revision, only the story about what it was being compared against.

## Addendum 3's "eval-time noise is harmless" claim does not survive either

Addendum 3 compared sampled vs. deterministic FVE on the same (buggy, saturated)
checkpoints and found a 0.000012 gap, concluding E2's damage was purely an
optimisation-path effect. Recomputed with corrected sigma (properly normalised
activations, a reconstruction-quality proxy on the same fixed batch, 5 seeds each,
not the full `loss_recovered` pipeline — flagged for official re-measurement
below):

| arm | FVE, sampling on | FVE, sampling off | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| `e2_sampling_only` | 0.3467 ± 0.0005 | 0.4705 ± 0.0007 | **0.124** |
| `e2_confirm` | 0.3252 ± 0.0005 | 0.4400 ± 0.0011 | **0.115** |

Consistent across all 10 seeds checked (5 each). **The eval-time noise is not
harmless** — turning it off recovers roughly 0.12 FVE on its own, ~10,000x
addendum 3's reported gap. E2's damage is not purely "in the weights"; a real
share of it is paid every time the sampled model is evaluated, because the sampled
model never stops sampling with real noise.

## The Jaccard-overlap instrumentation (the original task) now confirms Claims-worth-opening #3 cleanly

`e2_sampling_only_early` / `e2_sigma_low_init_early` (`falsification/run_arm.py`):
same two arms, 5 seeds each, with dense intermediate checkpointing
(`checkpoint_steps`, steps 0–8500) via trainSAE's `save_steps`. At each checkpoint,
`encode(x, return_topk=True, training=True)` twice on the same fixed batch of real
activations; mean per-token Jaccard overlap of the two top-k index sets is the
instability measure (`falsification/read_selection_jaccard.py`, bias-corrected as
above). Reference: two independent uniform-random 256-subsets of 2048 average
Jaccard 0.0667.

| step | 0 | 25 | 50 | 100 | 200 | 500 | 1000 | 2500 | 6000 | 10000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `e2_sampling_only_early` | 0.069 | 0.074 | 0.122 | 0.330 | 0.476 | 0.674 | 0.738 | 0.761 | 0.792 | **0.811** |
| `e2_sigma_low_init_early` | 0.431 | 0.485 | 0.583 | 0.774 | 0.890 | 0.924 | 0.935 | 0.945 | 0.951 | **0.952** |

Within-arm across-seed SDs are 0.0001–0.007 throughout (n=5) — every step's gap
between arms is many multiples of that. Two findings, and they land exactly where
the corrected sigma reading predicts:

* **`e2_sampling_only`'s selection starts at essentially chance level.** 0.069
  against a 0.0667 random-subset floor — at step 0 the reparameterisation noise
  (`sigma ≈ 0.368` initially, and it never collapses) so dominates `mu` that which
  features get selected is nearly indistinguishable from random, exactly the
  mechanism Claims-worth-opening #3 proposed.
* **It never fully stabilises.** Because the true sigma settles around 0.27
  rather than collapsing (this addendum, above), selection instability persists
  the whole of training: Jaccard is still only 0.811 at step 10000, meaning close
  to one in five selected features differs between two forward passes on the
  *same* token at convergence. `e2_sigma_low_init`, whose sigma is genuinely
  pinned at the floor throughout, reaches 0.952 — far more stable at every step
  from 0 onward, and the gap **never closes**.

This is the opposite conclusion from a first pass at this same measurement (using
the then-undiscovered-as-buggy checkpoints directly), which found the two arms
nearly identical at step 0 and `e2_sigma_low_init` very slightly *less* stable
through the middle of training — an artifact of both arms appearing
clamp-saturated from step 0 regardless of `log_var_init`, since the bug pins
`e2_sampling_only`'s *saved* bias far below the floor too. The corrected reading
reverses this cleanly: **selection instability tracks reconstruction quality in
the predicted direction, is largest exactly where FVE is worst, and both metrics
now agree that pinning sigma at the floor from step 0 is what buys the recovery.**
Even `e2_sigma_low_init` does not reach 0.952 by step 0 (0.431) — feature identity
still takes hundreds of steps to stabilise even under near-zero noise, which may
be the more general point: some selection churn at initialisation is unavoidable
because `mu` itself starts small and undifferentiated, independent of sigma.

## What this leaves open

* **E2's officially reported FVE for every other `var_flag=1` arm on disk**
  (`e2_confirm`, `e2_learned_var`, the `e2_beta_pilot_*` arms) was measured
  through the same buggy checkpoints and the standard `evaluate()` pipeline, which
  also reads raw activations with no correction. Given the eval-time gap measured
  above, these numbers plausibly **understate** how much sampling degrades
  reconstruction — `e2_sampling_only`'s reported 0.484 likely reads closer to a
  *softened* version of its true sampled behaviour. `e2_sigma_low_init`'s own
  reported numbers (addendum 7) are the one exception, unaffected because its
  sigma saturates the floor regardless of the bug — so addendum 7's central
  finding (84% of the gap to baseline closes) stands, and if anything the true gap
  it closes is larger than reported, not smaller, since the baseline it is closing
  against (`e2_sampling_only`) was itself measured too charitably.
* **No re-evaluation or retraining has been done in this addendum** beyond the
  proxy FVE check above and the Jaccard read — re-running the official
  `evaluate()`/`loss_recovered()` pipeline on bias-corrected copies of the
  existing `e2_confirm`/`e2_sampling_only`/`e2_learned_var`/`e2_beta_pilot_*`
  checkpoints (no retraining needed — only `var_encoder.bias` and
  `var_encoder.weight` need correcting, encoder/decoder are untouched by the bug)
  is the natural next step and would put a real, comparable number on the
  understatement.
